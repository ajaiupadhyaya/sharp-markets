\begin{abstract}
\noindent Sport has become the largest category on the new event-contract exchanges, yet
existing studies judge these markets only against realised outcomes. I compare them instead with
the benchmark sports bettors have long used: the Betfair Exchange closing line. Across
\nLeagues{} European football leagues in 2025--26, I match \nKalshiMatched{} Kalshi and
\nPmMatched{} Polymarket match-winner markets to fixtures and compare their kickoff
probabilities with Betfair's closing odds, match by match. In this exploratory season the
paired log-score difference is \rqOneMeanK{} nats per match for Kalshi (s.e.\ \rqOneSeK) and
\rqOneMeanP{} for Polymarket (s.e.\ \rqOneSeP). Both are statistically indistinguishable from
Betfair, while the ordinary bookmaker average is clearly worse (\rqOneMeanAvg, s.e.\
\rqOneSeAvg). Read at kickoff, both venues' probabilities are slightly more extreme than
Betfair's. Most of that gap disappears when the venues are read 30--60 minutes earlier, so it
reflects late information rather than a pricing bias. The paper's confirmatory hypothesis was
registered before any of its data existed: on 2026--27 matches played after the plan was
tagged, Kalshi's probabilities are within $\pm 0.002$ nats per match of the Betfair close,
tested by two one-sided tests. That result will be added after the window closes on 30 June
2027. Nothing in this paper is betting or investment advice.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Since mid-2025, most of the volume on Kalshi, the CFTC-regulated event-contract exchange, and on
Polymarket, its crypto-settled rival, has been sport \citep{pew2026}. That raises a plain
question with real money behind it: do these markets price a football match as well as the
markets that have priced sport for decades?

The calibration literature that has grown up around the new venues doesn't answer it.
\citet{burgi2025} show that Kalshi's prices are fairly accurate, become more accurate as markets
approach their close, and display a favourite--longshot bias. \citet{cardozo2026} find the bias
across 588 million Polymarket trades, except in sport. \citet{moshrefi2026} and \citet{le2026}
study calibration by horizon and domain. Every one of these studies compares prices with
realised frequencies. None compares them with the \emph{closing line}, the last price of the
sharpest pre-existing market and the standard benchmark in sports betting. \citet{diercks2026}
make exactly this kind of benchmark comparison for Kalshi's macroeconomic contracts against fed
funds futures and surveys. This paper does it for sport.

The design is paired: two venues price the same outcome of the same match at the same moment.
Most match-level noise cancels, and every season brings thousands of independent matches. The
precision that results is very high. In the 2025--26 exploratory season the standard error of
Kalshi's mean log-score difference from Betfair is \rqOneSeK{} nats per match. That is small
enough to detect a gap a third the size of the one separating ordinary bookmakers from the
exchange.

The paper is a registered report. Everything in Section~\ref{sec:explore} comes from 2025--26,
was looked at freely, and is exploratory. The single confirmatory hypothesis
(Section~\ref{sec:design}) was fixed in a pre-analysis plan before any of its data existed. Its
sample is every 2026--27 match kicking off after the plan's git tag. Its analysis runs once,
through a script whose code is bound to that tag. Section~\ref{sec:confirm} says what will be
reported there.

\section{Related work}

\citet{wolfers2004} survey prediction markets as forecasting tools. The favourite--longshot bias
(longshots overpriced, favourites underpriced) is one of the oldest regularities in betting
markets. \citet{snowberg2010} attribute it to misperception of small probabilities rather than to
risk-love. On the new venues, \citet{burgi2025} document it on Kalshi across contract types,
with takers losing about 32\% on average and makers about 10\%. \citet{cardozo2026} find a
two-sided pattern on Polymarket in crypto and politics that is absent in sport, and show that its
sign depends on how related contracts are grouped. \citet{moshrefi2026} reports that Kalshi's
sports calibration becomes step-like in the final minutes, and \citet{le2026} finds
horizon-dependent bias in Polymarket sports beyond a week. Across venues, \citet{ng2025} find
two-way price discovery between Polymarket and Kalshi, with Kalshi leading in sport, and
\citet{gebele2026} document law-of-one-price deviations of 2--4\% across ten venues.
\citet{kanoria2025} estimate that about a quarter of Polymarket's reported volume is wash
trading, which matters here because volume is the natural liquidity measure.

The methods are standard:
\begin{itemize}
  \item log and Brier scores, which are strictly proper \citep{gneiting2007};
  \item paired score differences, compared as in \citet{diebold1995};
  \item equivalence tested by two one-sided tests \citep{schuirmann1987,lakens2017};
  \item cluster-robust standard errors \citep{cameron2015};
  \item bookmaker margins removed proportionally or by \citet{shin1993}'s method;
  \item information content tested with a logarithmic opinion pool \citep{genest1986}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Data}\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Fixtures and the closing line.} Results and closing 1X2 odds come from
Football-Data.co.uk \citep{footballdata}: the \nLeagues{} divisions in Table~\ref{tab:coverage},
\nFixtures{} league fixtures in 2025--26. Betfair Exchange closing odds are available for
\nBetfair{} of them, and for the 2026--27 season. Pinnacle closing odds cover only about 40\% of
2025--26 and none of 2026--27, so Pinnacle appears only as a yardstick. Kickoff times are
converted from UK time to UTC.

\paragraph{Kalshi.} The public API lists \nKalshiEvents{} settled match events in these leagues
for 2025--26, each with three markets (home, away, tie). For each market I read the minute
candles over the three hours before kickoff. The price is the mid of the closing yes-bid and
yes-ask in the last candle at or before kickoff. When the book is one-sided or wider than ten
cents, the price falls back to the last trade; that happened for \kFallback{} fixture. At kickoff
the median spread is \kSpread{} cents and the median volume is \kVolume{} contracts per match.

\paragraph{Polymarket.} The Gamma API lists \nPmEvents{} closed full-time-result events. Each has
two ``Will X win on DATE?'' markets and one draw market; halftime, spread and other side markets
are excluded. The price is the last point of the CLOB price history at or before kickoff.

\paragraph{Matching.} Each venue event is matched by normalised team-name similarity to a
football-data fixture in the same division within a day, subject to a minimum score and a margin
over the runner-up. \nKalshiMatched{} Kalshi and \nPmMatched{} Polymarket events match (98\%).
Fixtures where the venue's settlement disagrees with football-data's result are dropped (4 for
Kalshi, 6 for Polymarket). Every source's three prices are divided by their sum.

\section{Measures and estimators}\label{sec:measures}

For fixture $i$ with result $r_i \in \{H, D, A\}$, the paired log-score difference between a
venue $v$ and Betfair is
\begin{equation}
d_i = \log p_{v,i}(r_i) - \log p_{\mathrm{BF},i}(r_i),
\end{equation}
which is positive when the venue was more accurate. Brier-score differences are defined with the
sign reversed, so a positive value still favours the venue. Standard errors of means are CR1
cluster-robust by division $\times$ ISO week, because matches on the same weekend in the same
league share shocks. In practice, clustering changes the standard error very little.

Calibration (the favourite--longshot bias) is measured by the slope $b$ in
$\operatorname{logit}\Pr(y=1) = a + b \operatorname{logit}(p)$ over the three outcome rows of
each match. $b > 1$ means favourites are underpriced and longshots overpriced. Venue and Betfair
slopes are fitted jointly on the same rows, so their difference has a clustered standard error.

Information content is tested with the pool
$\Pr(o) \propto \exp\{\beta_{\mathrm{BF}} \log p_{\mathrm{BF}}(o) + \beta_v \log p_v(o)\}$,
fitted by maximum likelihood. $\beta_v = 0$ means Betfair encompasses the venue.

\subsection{Timing: the finding of the exploratory season}

Read at kickoff, both venues' calibration slopes are slightly \emph{lower} than Betfair's on the
same rows: by \gapZeroK{} for Kalshi and \gapZeroP{} for Polymarket. Their probabilities are a
little more extreme. The natural explanation is timing. The venues are read in the last minute,
Betfair's ``closing'' snapshot is taken some time before kickoff, and the last half hour brings
team news.

Reading the venues earlier, from the same candles, tests that explanation
(Table~\ref{tab:timing}, Figure~\ref{fig:timing}). Polymarket's gap is gone 30--60 minutes before
kickoff (\gapSixtyP{} at $-60$ minutes). Kalshi's shrinks by about 40\% but persists
(\gapSixtyK{}, s.e.\ \gapSixtySeK{}, at $-60$ minutes). Accuracy barely moves with the read time.

The timing pattern is the result I report from this season: \emph{the last half hour before
kickoff makes prediction-market prices more extreme than the exchange's close}. The persistent
part of Kalshi's gap is smaller than one season can detect at a timing-matched read (minimum
detectable gap about 0.014). It is reported as a pattern, not tested.

\section{Pre-registered confirmatory design}\label{sec:design}

The pre-analysis plan (\texttt{docs/pap/pre-analysis-plan.md}) was signed and frozen as the
annotated git tag \texttt{pap-v1} at 2026-10-25 19:22:02 UTC (T0). The repository and the tag
were pushed to GitHub 84 seconds later.

\paragraph{Hypothesis.} \textbf{H1: at kickoff, Kalshi's match probabilities are as accurate as
the Betfair Exchange closing line, to within $\delta = 0.002$ nats per match.} The null is
non-equivalence, $|E[d]| \geq \delta$. It is tested by two one-sided tests at 5\% each, with the
clustered standard error of Section~\ref{sec:measures}. Equivalence is declared if and only if
the 90\% confidence interval lies inside $(-\delta, \delta)$. The margin is about the gap between
ordinary bookmakers and the exchange in 2025--26, so the claim reads: Kalshi is closer to the
exchange than ordinary bookmakers are. The plan discloses that the equivalence framing was chosen
after seeing 2025--26.

\paragraph{Sample.} Every league fixture in the \nLeagues{} divisions that kicks off after T0
and on or before 30 June 2027, and is priced by both Kalshi and Betfair. If fewer than 1,500 such
fixtures exist, H1 is reported as exploratory. Polymarket (H2) is secondary, as are the
robustness variants of Table~\ref{tab:robust}.

\paragraph{Single run.} The script \texttt{sharpmkt.confirm} runs once, after the window closes.
It refuses to run unless \texttt{pap-v1} is an annotated tag that HEAD descends from and every
file under \texttt{src/}, plus \texttt{pyproject.toml} and \texttt{uv.lock}, equals its blob at
the tag. It then:
\begin{itemize}
  \item creates an attempt marker by exclusive creation;
  \item pulls the 2026--27 data itself, which is the first read of the confirmatory outcomes;
  \item keeps the fixtures inside the window;
  \item writes its output once.
\end{itemize}
Every pipeline module refuses the 2026--27 season unless this script started it.

\paragraph{Power.} Table~\ref{tab:power} gives H1's power if the true difference is zero, by the
share of the season remaining after T0. About four-fifths of 2026--27 remained at signing.

\section{Discussion and limitations}

On the exploratory evidence, the new prediction markets price European football about as well
as the sharpest exchange, and better than ordinary bookmakers. That is a strong result for venues
a few years old, and the mechanism may be simple. If their market makers quote off the exchange,
the venues will match it almost by construction; the collinearity in Table~\ref{tab:rq2} is
consistent with that. Being as sharp as the exchange is not the same as adding information to
it. This design can show the first, and the data can't yet show the second.

Several limits apply:
\begin{itemize}
  \item football-data's closing snapshot isn't time-stamped to the minute, which is why the
    timing analysis matters;
  \item coverage differs by venue and grew during the season;
  \item Polymarket's volume is partly wash trading;
  \item venue settlement occasionally disagrees with the official result;
  \item the confirmatory test concerns one season of one sport.
\end{itemize}
