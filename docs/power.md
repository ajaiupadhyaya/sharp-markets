# Power check (2025-26, exploratory)

2025–26 is the exploratory season: everything here may be looked at, and nothing in it is
confirmatory. The confirmatory sample is 2026–27 matches kicking off after the pre-analysis plan
is signed. The tables between the markers are generated by `uv run python -m sharpmkt.power`;
this text is not.

## Verdict

**RQ1 is well powered. The paired design does what the Italy study couldn't.**

- **Precision.** For Kalshi against the Betfair Exchange closing line, the clustered standard
  error of the mean log-score difference is **0.0004 nats per match** (3,426 matches). The MDE
  is about **0.0012 nats** over a full season, 0.0016 if the confirmatory window is 60% of a
  season, and 0.0019 at 40%. Polymarket: about 0.0017 / 0.0022 / 0.0027 (3,851 matches).
- **What that size means.** The yardstick is the **bookmaker average**. Ordinary bookmakers'
  closing prices are worse than Betfair's by **0.0022 ± 0.0005 nats**, clearly non-zero, as
  expected. That's a useful check that the pipeline is sound. Even a 40% confirmatory window can
  detect a gap smaller than the one separating ordinary bookmakers from the exchange. Pinnacle
  (2,634 matches) sits at −0.0018 ± 0.0011.
- **Clustering barely matters** (clustered se ≈ iid se). The paired difference cancels weekend
  and league shocks, which is why the precision is so high.

**What 2025–26 shows (exploratory, not a finding).** Kalshi (−0.0003 ± 0.0004) and Polymarket
(−0.0005 ± 0.0006) are statistically indistinguishable from Betfair, and both look better than
the bookmaker average. At kickoff, Kalshi's book is tight: median spread 1.3 cents, a quote in
the last minute, and a median of about 131k contracts per match.

**What that means for the plan.** The likely confirmatory answer is a **precise zero**, so RQ1
should be pre-registered as an **equivalence test** (two one-sided tests), not only a difference
test. The natural margin is the bookmaker-average gap (about 0.002 nats): "Kalshi is within the
distance that separates ordinary bookmakers from the exchange." With an se of 0.0004–0.0007, the
equivalence test has room to pass, even in a 40% window.

**Caveats to settle before signing.**
- **Timing.** football-data's closing odds are collected near kickoff, not exactly at it, while
  Kalshi and Polymarket are read in the last minute. If anything, that favours the venues,
  which makes "no better than Betfair" more telling, but the plan has to state it.
- **Coverage is uneven.** Kalshi has no National League or Ligue 2 and thin League One; Polymarket
  has no Belgium or Greece. The plan should fix the league list per venue in advance.
- **Kalshi's mid falls back to the last trade** when the book is one-sided or wider than 10
  cents. That's rare at kickoff, but the plan should report how often it happens.
- **Matching.** 98% of venue events match a fixture. 63 Kalshi events didn't (mostly name
  variants such as Gil Vicente and Rayo Vallecano, and playoff matches football-data doesn't
  carry). Settlement disagrees with football-data for 4 Kalshi and 6 Polymarket fixtures, which
  are dropped.

**Exploratory findings worth flagging (2025–26 only, not confirmatory):**
- **RQ3.** Both venues' calibration slopes are a little *lower* than Betfair's: Kalshi −0.018
  (se 0.003), Polymarket −0.013 (se 0.003). Their probabilities are slightly more spread out
  (more extreme) than Betfair's on the same outcomes. The likely innocent explanation is
  **timing**: the venues are read in the last minute, and late information such as team news
  makes prices more extreme. The plan has to rule that out before this can be called a bias, for
  example by reading the venues at the same clock time as the football-data snapshot.
- **RQ3, returns by price.** Longshots under 10% lose 23% (Kalshi) and 35% (Polymarket) per unit
  staked, before fees, which is the classic favourite–longshot pattern. With only about 330 rows
  per venue, the se is about 0.16–0.18, so it's noise this season.
- **RQ4.** No division stands out once its se is taken into account.

## Timing control (added 2026-10-25)

I read each venue at kickoff and 30, 60 and 120 minutes before it, from the same candles, on the
fixtures priced at every offset (Kalshi 3,255, Polymarket 3,850). Betfair's snapshot stays
fixed. Quotes are live at every offset (median staleness 0–1 minutes).

| | read at kickoff | −30 min | −60 min | −120 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kalshi** RQ3 slope gap vs Betfair | −0.018 (0.003) | −0.010 (0.003) | −0.011 (0.005) | −0.013 (0.005) |
| **Polymarket** RQ3 slope gap | −0.012 (0.003) | −0.004 (0.003) | −0.001 (0.005) | −0.001 (0.005) |
| Kalshi RQ1 mean d | −0.0004 (0.0004) | −0.0003 (0.0006) | −0.0009 (0.0008) | −0.0011 (0.0009) |
| Polymarket RQ1 mean d | −0.0005 (0.0006) | +0.0000 (0.0006) | +0.0000 (0.0008) | −0.0004 (0.0008) |

**What it shows.**
- **Polymarket's gap is timing.** It disappears once Polymarket is read 30–60 minutes before
  kickoff. So Betfair's "closing" snapshot behaves like a price from roughly that window, and
  the last half hour (team news) is what makes Polymarket's prices more extreme. Not a bias.
- **Kalshi's gap is partly timing and partly not.** It shrinks by about 40% (−0.018 to about
  −0.011) but stays at roughly 2–2.5 se at −60 and −120 minutes. Kalshi's probabilities are
  slightly more extreme than Betfair's even an hour or two out, which is where a real
  structural difference would show (for example, a bias carried over from US sportsbook-style
  traders or from how Kalshi's market makers quote).
- **RQ1 doesn't move.** Reading an hour earlier makes no detectable difference to accuracy
  (paired change +0.0005 ± 0.0008 Kalshi, −0.0006 ± 0.0007 Polymarket). The equivalence result
  doesn't depend on read time.

**The catch for power.** At −60 minutes the slope-gap se is 0.005, not 0.003, so the MDE is
about **0.014 over a full season and 0.022 in a 40% window**. The persistent Kalshi gap of about
−0.011 is *below* that. A timing-matched RQ3 would probably not confirm it in one season. The
kickoff-read RQ3 is well powered, but timing contaminates it.

**Revised plan shape.**
1. **Confirmatory:** RQ1 as an equivalence test (margin about 0.002 nats) for Kalshi and
   Polymarket, read at kickoff, with −60 minutes as a pre-registered robustness read. Timing
   doesn't move RQ1, so this is safe.
2. **RQ3: exploratory (decided 2026-10-25).** The finding to report is the timing pattern: read
   at kickoff, both venues' prices are more extreme than Betfair's close, and the gap shrinks
   (Polymarket: vanishes) when they are read 30–60 minutes earlier. The persistent Kalshi part
   is reported with its MDE, not as a test.
3. **Secondary:** RQ4 liquidity slope, RQ2 for Polymarket.
4. **Dropped:** RQ2 for Kalshi.
